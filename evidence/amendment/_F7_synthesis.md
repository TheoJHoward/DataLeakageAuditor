## 1. THE DENOMINATOR FINDING — **CONFIRMED**, in its narrow form. Report 4's refutation fails on the point that matters.

Report 4 refutes a claim the finding does not make. Its R3 defends the **partition of the declared scored set** — correctly. SC-4(b) is exhaustive ("**There is no fourth class and no residue class**"), every class carries a consequence, and moving a unit REQUIRED → OUT OF JURISDICTION trades *absence is a miss* for *a finding on it fails the gate*. That much is sound and the UNSCORED-escape route is genuinely closed by SC-4(d)(ii), SC-4(e), SC-6(b), SC-3(c) and SC-11. **The "shrink after a result" and "free class" mechanisms are dead.**

But the finding is about the **floor under the set, and the absence of any anchor outside the declaration** — and Report 4 concedes the whole of it in its own opening paragraph: *"no clause of §6.2 as composed states a minimum N, a non-emptiness test on the REQUIRED list, or a numeric floor on the declared scored population."* Three things make that concession decisive rather than academic:

- **SC-4(g) lets a unit escape the partition entirely, before any class is assigned:** *"a unit the declaration does not feed to the scored pipeline holds **no gate class whatever***." R3's unit-for-unit trade only operates on units inside the declared scored set. Nothing registered fixes what enters it.
- **SC-3(c) hands the set to the declaration:** *"The declaration declares the scored population — the rows and units the criteria adjudicate."*
- **SC-4(a) bars the only independent object from checking it:** *"**No classification of the scored set other than this derivation enters any criterion, denominator, or count**."* Report 4's R2 answer — that SC-4(a) contemplates the manifest count being *quoted*, and that §A.6.4 in fact reconciles 11+22+2 = 35 against the manifest — is right and is not enough. That reconciliation is disclosure without consequence, and it is voluntary: §A.6.5 says of itself *"it derives nothing, changes no class, and **is not a gate object**."*

Report 4's honest fallback — R4, that SC-8(d) is *"a motive rule and is not machine-checkable"*, and R6, that §10.2 criterion 2 (*"cannot separate contaminated from corrected → **stop**"*) is the usefulness backstop — is where the refutation actually rests. **R6 does not hold as the diff is currently written.** See §5 item 1: hunk 2.33's operative text deletes that sentence on the branch that governs this fixture. Remove R6 and Report 4 has a motive rule and nothing else.

Two corrections to Report 4, both against it: it cites SC-8(d) correctly (Report 1's "SC-9(d)" is wrong — the clause is **SC-8(d)**, `_E3_composed_sections.md` L426); and its R1 reading of SC-4(j) is correct — *"two sets of equal size are not thereby the same set"* is anti-substitution, and SC-4(f)(1)/(3) require enumerated lists and a printed partition check. That limb of the claim is properly refuted.

**Smallest closing clause.** One limb, **SC-4(k)**, placed at the end of the SC-4 block (composed L172, after (j)), modelled verbatim on the sentence the schema set already contains at SC-13b(b1):

> **(k) THE REQUIRED LIST IS NON-EMPTY, AND ITS POPULATION IS ANCHORED.** The declared scored set and the REQUIRED list are enumerated by name before any detector runs and are **non-empty on every declared side**. If either is empty on any declared side, criterion 1 is not discharged and the outcome is **STOP**, lifted only by supplementing the declaration and re-freezing under §11. The REQUIRED list is published alongside a **per-unit reconciliation against the manifest's independently-leaking-source list**, every difference named with the registered predicate of (b) that produced it. **A reconciliation published under this limb is a disclosure, not a classification entering a criterion, denominator, or count** (SC-4(a)).

The final sentence is required: as composed, SC-4(a) forbids the very check that closes the hole. The instance this would have caught is already on the record and unresolved unit-by-unit — the manifest records `independently_leaking_sources: 25`, N is 11, and fourteen of the difference sit mostly in the 22-unit OOJ class where a finding on them is a **false positive**.

## 2. THE UNSCORED-PRECEDENCE FINDING — **REFUTED as a live hole; CONFIRMED as an unclosed enumeration.** Do not sign without the one sentence.

Report 5 wins on the mechanism and overreaches on the closure.

**Wins, and I adopt them.** The premise "predicates not of the same kind" is false — all three rows of SC-4(b) read `declares`/`declared`, so REQUIRED is *"the map declares a violation on it"*, not "the fixture leaks". **Ground 1 is the contradictory of REQUIRED** and can never co-fire with it. SC-4(d)(ii) (*"unconstructibility in some other rebuild of the fixture is never gate-unscoredness"*) and SC-6(c) (*"A cell-level `unscored` never makes its unit `unscored`"*) block the level-shifting versions. And **SC-9(e) is the clause that actually kills the invented-ground route** — verified verbatim: *"Any interpretation that weakens a locked obligation — **narrows a denominator, exempts a unit**, softens a criterion, admits an excluded set … is a class C amendment and may not be recorded as a working resolution, a decision-log entry, or a reading. This binds … **the reading of this registration by its own author**."* A third exclusion ground narrows the denominator; it is therefore not a declaration act.

**Overreach, and I adjudicate against Report 5.** Its argument 2 says SC-4(a) closes (e)'s grounds. SC-4(a) does not reach (e). Read exactly, it closes *"the class predicates of (b), under the precedence of (c)"* — and (b)'s UNSCORED predicate is *"scoring on it is declared impossible, **on a ground the declaration states**."* The predicate's **form** is registered; its **content** is delegated to (e), and (e) says only *"Two grounds are registered here because …"* — a statement of fact about the clause, not a closure. Report 5 concedes this ("The claim's one true observation") and then leans on `SCHEMA_SET_FINAL.md` L136's classification of the numeral as a closure constraint — which is drafting-record reasoning, not registered text. Closure therefore rests on a two-hop inference through SC-9(e) at a site where the amendment's own house style states closure in one line (SC-12(w1): *"the enumeration is **closed**, and it has **no members**"*).

Also unaddressed by Report 5: **ground 2 is a real, registered exit from REQUIRED that the declaration controls** — *"a unit whose **construction or lag treatment is declared UNRESOLVED**"*. Its guards are ex-ante freeze (SC-8(b)/(c)), a motive rule (SC-8(d)), and SC-3(h)'s *"The unscored disposition is not an escape hatch"* — which, as round 1 said and I confirm, is a conclusion with no test, no adjudicator and no consequence.

**Smallest closing clause.** Append one sentence to SC-4(e), composed L136:

> **The enumeration of grounds is closed: a ground not registered here is not a ground, and adding one is a class C amendment.**

Costless, removes an argument entirely, and matches SC-12(w1)'s form.
