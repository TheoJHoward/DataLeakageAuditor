# Figures typed by hand in the documentation — the list

Nothing here is a `PREREG.md` §6.2 result and no figure here belongs beside the
Phase 1 acceptance figures.

**The ask.** R228 §1: *which other figures in the documentation are typed by hand
and could be emitted instead? Report the list; generate nothing this round.*
Nothing is generated here. R229 §1(c) adds that the floor row is the most
important member of it.

**The mechanism, stated once.** A figure a person copies is stale from the moment
it is written. Two things in the last two rounds point the same way: the pin that
works lives in the instrument — `check_registration.py` prints its own interpreter
on line two, so a figure copied out of it carries its version whether or not
anyone remembered — and the pin that failed lived in the operator's habit. **The
frame travels with the figure, emitted by the thing that measures it, or it does
not travel.**

**The evidence that this is not theoretical.** The README's contrast pair has now
gone stale *twice inside single rounds*: 598 → 622 within R226, and 622 → 646
within R229 — both times written by hand, both times wrong within hours, both
times because tests were added by the same round that quoted the number.

## What the list says

**Two closed, three open, one that must stay typed, three already emitted.** The
open ones are all in `README.md`'s verification section and share one source: they
report what the suite and the gate do at a commit, and nothing runs them.

**#6 is the interesting entry**, because it marks the boundary of the rule. "Emit
rather than type" is right for a figure that *describes the current tree* and
wrong for one that *records a past measurement*. The acceptance figures, and the
dated environment rows in `INSTALL.md`, are the second kind — which is why the
retired digest was marked retired **in place** rather than overwritten with its
replacement. That distinction was got wrong once during R229 and corrected: the
new digest was briefly written into the dated rows, which would have made five
historical measurements assert a number none of them produced.

**#9 is the next one worth doing** and is not on the R228 list because it is not
documentation: the whole-frame guard is the project's most-quoted instrument and
lives in a session scratch directory, which is precisely the property that made
the retired digest unreproducible.
